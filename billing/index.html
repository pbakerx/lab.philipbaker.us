The Problem

Billing Was Always the Thing That Got Ignored

Here's the thing nobody tells you about freelancing: the actual work — the creative direction, the design, the animation — that part is great. You stay up until 2am because you're in the zone. But billing? Billing is the thing you do when you're already tired, after you've spent all your creative energy, and the last thing you want to do is open QuickBooks.

And the irony isn't lost on me. Billing is literally the whole point. It's how you get paid. But if it's not exciting, it doesn't get done. Invoices would pile up. Timesheets would go untracked — I'd finish a 3-hour session and think "I'll log that later" and later would be never. Money was left on the table. Not because the work wasn't done, but because I couldn't bring myself to document it.

Before

  • Timesheets logged days (or weeks) late, if at all
  • Invoices created in batches when guilt finally hit
  • No idea who owed what or how overdue it was
  • Bank transactions piling up uncategorized

  • Billing rates tracked in my head, not a system
  • Hours lost to the QuickBooks UI every month

After

  • Timesheets spoken into existence from my phone
  • AI creates invoices the moment work is logged
  • AR aging tracked and surfaced automatically
  • 900+ transactions categorized by AI in one session
  • 29 months reconciled and caught up
  • Per-client rates encoded in custom AI skills
  • Full billing cycle runs from a conversation
The Moment It Clicked

What If Billing Was Just a Conversation?

The whole thing started with a simple idea: what if I could just tell someone what I worked on, and everything else happened automatically? Not an app with forms and dropdowns and save buttons. Just… talking.

Now I speak my timesheets into Dispatch while I'm walking to my car after a session. The AI knows the billing rate for every client, which service item each one bills under, and the quirks that live nowhere but in my head — which clients carry a different internal and invoiced rate, and which ones pay in a form that needs special handling on the accounting side. I don't look any of it up anymore.

The Ledger

What the System Actually Sees

The same API connection that creates invoices also reads back. That means the AI isn't just a data-entry shortcut at the front of the process — it can answer questions about the state of the business at any point, without me opening a single report.

I can ask who's overdue and by how long, what a given client has been billed this year, which invoices are still unpaid, or what a month looked like compared to the one before it. Those used to be questions I avoided asking, because finding the answer meant an evening in QuickBooks. Now they're just questions.

The Catch-Up

29 Months of "I'll Do It Later"

The timesheet-to-invoice pipeline was just the beginning. When I finally connected the AI to my QuickBooks, the real scale of the backlog became clear: 29 months of unreconciled bank statements and 900+ uncategorized transactions sitting in the feed.

Manually, that's weeks of work — opening each transaction, figuring out the category, checking against bank records, matching deposits to invoices. The AI did it conversationally. We worked through it together: it would surface batches of transactions, I'd confirm the categorization patterns, and it would apply them across hundreds of entries. It even caught duplicate Apple Cash deposits that would have thrown off the books.

What would have taken me weeks of dreading, procrastinating, and eventually grinding through — the AI handled in working sessions that felt more like a conversation than data entry.

How It Works

The Stack Behind the Scenes

This isn't a custom app or a startup product. It's a personal AI workflow built with tools that exist today, connected to the accounting software I was already paying for.

Claude + Custom Skills

AI that understands my billing rules, client rates, tax compliance requirements, and QBO-specific quirks like owner's draw handling.

Dispatch (Mobile)

Voice-to-text on my phone. I speak my timesheets on the go — the AI parses client, project, hours, and description.

QuickBooks Online API

Direct API connection for creating invoices, tracking AR, managing customers, and pulling real-time financial data.

Custom Billing Skills

Encoded business logic: per-client rates, service categories, tax rules, invoice routing, and the marked-up-rate workflow.

Bank Reconciliation

AI-assisted transaction categorization and bank statement matching. Caught duplicates and resolved 29 months of backlog.

AR Monitoring

Automated aging reports, overdue alerts, and past-due statement generation. Always knows who owes what.

The Takeaway

The Real Change Isn't Efficiency — It's That It Actually Gets Done

I could talk about time saved or automation or efficiency gains. But that misses the point. The real change is simpler than that: billing actually happens now.

Before, I had a perfectly functional QuickBooks account. I had invoice templates. I knew how to create a timesheet. The tools were fine. The problem was me — I'm a creative person, and administrative work will always lose the priority battle against a project that's actually interesting.

The AI didn't replace QuickBooks. It replaced the friction. The logging in, the clicking through, the context-switching from creative work to spreadsheet work. It turned billing from a chore I dreaded into a sentence I say on my way to the car.

And the backlog — the 29 months of unreconciled statements, the hundreds of uncategorized transactions — that wasn't a technology problem either. It was an "I really don't want to do this" problem. The AI made it possible to chip away at it conversationally, without it feeling like punishment.

If you're a freelancer or a small business owner and your billing looks like mine used to, you don't need a better app. You need to remove yourself from the process as much as possible. That's what this does.
