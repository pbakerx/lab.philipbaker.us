Ackerman McQueen

am.com  ·  1997 – 1999  ·  designed by Philip Baker

Before the Shockwave gateway and the Flash rebuild, am.com was this: black to the edges, hairline teal rules, small-caps navigation, photography doing the talking. I designed it in the late '90s, when an agency website was still a thing you invented from scratch. The server is long gone — these are the surviving pages, recovered from the Wayback Machine, shown as they actually rendered.

What The Place Was

These screenshots can't explain what it was actually like inside. Everything was about advertising. Everything was about being the best. Nothing went out the door unless it had been absolutely scrutinized — mounted, flung, and every single piece of creative sent to Angus McQueen's chair for approval. I blocked tapes. I edited on the three-quarter-inch machine, and then the Avid. I cried plenty of tears there, and I miss it — the craft of iteration, how cool things were back then. That place shaped me.

Angus McQueen was a fierce man and the best mentor I've ever had. He wasn't the only one. Jon Minson, Gayle Daniels, Jeanette Elliott, Debbie Johnson, Lael Erickson, Melanie Hill, Clay Turner, David Elmore, Rick Perdue, Steve Elmore, Gurney Sloan, Barbara Johnston, Tim Oden — all of them took time they didn't have to teach me. I felt special. I still don't know how they found the time.

This page is for them.
