    📚 Feed it

    Everything it will ever know comes from this text. Nothing else goes in.

    ✎ You changed the text. Press Learn from this text to use it. Until you do, it keeps reading the old one.

    Three ways to feed it: click one of the ready-made texts above, paste your own into the box (song lyrics, a recipe book, your emails) and press Learn from this text, or upload a plain-text file (.txt). A few pages is plenty. New text means a fresh brain, because its alphabet changes.

    💡
    How the real ones workA model can only echo what it was fed, which is why what goes into training is the biggest decision there is. Feed it dinosaurs and it dreams in dinosaurs. The big labs spend enormous effort choosing and cleaning their text for exactly this reason.

    🔧 Tinker with training: step size, batch, speed, optimizer

    These change how it learns and apply instantly, even mid-training. No rebuild needed.

    Step size

    How big each nudge is (the learning rate). Too small and it learns slowly. Too big and it overshoots, and the loss jumps around or explodes. Try dragging it all the way right.

    Batch

    How many snippets it reads before each nudge. More snippets give a steadier sense of direction, but each step takes longer.

    Speed

    Steps per screen refresh. This only changes how fast you watch; it doesn't change what it learns.

    Optimizer

    ▶ Train runs the loop continuously (Space bar works too). Step ×1 runs it exactly once, so you can watch a single nudge.

    💡
    How the real ones workThis is how every chatbot was made. Guess the next piece of text, measure how wrong, nudge the numbers. The only difference is scale: they read a large slice of the internet and nudge hundreds of billions of numbers, on thousands of chips for months. You're doing it with about 28,000 numbers in a browser tab.

    🧩 Build it

    The shape of its brain. Changing any of these means a brand-new, untrained brain: you'll train it from scratch.

    Layers

    How many times it re-reads and rethinks the text before guessing. Deeper can learn subtler patterns.

    Heads

    Attention heads per layer: separate "spotlights" that can each look back at different earlier letters.

    Width

    How many numbers describe each character inside its head. Wider holds more detail.

    Memory

    How many recent characters it can see at once (its context window). Anything further back, it has forgotten.

    Throws away everything it learned and starts over with fresh random numbers, at the size you set.

    💡
    How the real ones workThese are the same knobs the labs turn. GPT-3 had 96 layers, 96 heads per layer, a width of 12,288 and 175 billion numbers. Modern chatbots remember hundreds of thousands of word-pieces at once. Yours has a memory of a couple dozen letters.

    Its writing right now
    Turn a head off or on and watch this change.

    If its guesses and writing get worse, that head was doing important work. If nothing changes, it wasn't pulling its weight. Tip: choose Copycat in Feed it, train for a minute, and come back. One head does most of the work there.

    🧠
    How the real ones workThis is brain surgery, and it's exactly how researchers study real AI. They switch parts off one at a time (they call it ablation) and see what breaks. Train on Copycat and one head learns to find the earlier copy of a word and look at the letter that came after it. Researchers at Anthropic named that pattern the induction head, and in 2022 showed it's a big part of how large models learn from examples in your prompt. Your tiny model discovers it on its own.

    🏔
    How the real ones workA real chatbot's space has hundreds of billions of directions, and nobody can picture that. So researchers do exactly this: take a flat slice through two random directions and paint the hills. This method comes from a 2018 paper on seeing why some networks train easily and others don't. The green arrow is the downhill direction, what training calls the gradient. Every step of learning is just "go that way a little."

    💡
    How the real ones workThere are no rules in here, no dictionary, no list of dinosaurs. Just numbers. That's why AI is hard to explain: a chatbot is hundreds of billions of these squares, and a whole research field (interpretability) is trying to read them. Scrambling a part and watching what breaks is one of its basic tools.
